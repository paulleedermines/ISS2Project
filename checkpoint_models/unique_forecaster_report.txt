WHY THIS IS A DIFFERENT FRAMEWORK

The inputs are sample indices. A Gaussian-process prior specifies a dense
covariance across time, combining a squared-exponential/RBF covariance and
a smooth periodic covariance, plus independent Gaussian observation noise:

 k(d) = signalVariance * [(1-w)*exp(-d^2/(2*ell^2))
                         + w*exp(-2*sin(pi*d/P)^2)]
 K = k + signalVariance*noiseRatio*I.

The GP posterior conditions on ALL revealed test values. It does not learn
a finite-order symbol-transition table or fit constant coefficients in a
finite lag equation. The RBF covariance does not impose finite-order Markov
dependence. A dense Cholesky factor gives causal Gaussian means/variances;
the Gaussian density sampled at categories 1:9 is normalized to a PMF,
matching the assignment's AR-to-symbol conversion.

The precise distinction is from the assignments' finite-order MC and
classical AR families. A GP conditional mean can itself be written as a
time-varying weighted sum of previous observations; all sequential joint
models also admit a product of conditional probabilities. Thus "not AR"
must not be interpreted as "never uses past observations" or as denying
these mathematical representations. It is a covariance-modeling framework.

The GP regression and covariance formulas follow Rasmussen and Williams,
Gaussian Processes for Machine Learning (2006), Chapters 2 and 4:
https://gaussianprocess.org/gpml/chapters/RW2.pdf
https://gaussianprocess.org/gpml/chapters/RW4.pdf
The experiment implements its own small exact GP; no GP library is needed.

TRAINING, SELECTION, AND CAUSALITY

The first 350 training symbols set the empirical mean and signal variance.
The last 150 training symbols select hyperparameters by mean symbol log
loss, using a reset validation history, exactly as the test run resets its
history. The prespecified GP grid is lengthscale [1,2,4,8], noise variance
ratio [0.01,0.03,0.1,0.3], period [12,24,48], and periodic weight [0,0.5,0.9].
All 144 combinations are recorded in unique_forecaster_gp_validation.csv.
The winner is ell=4, noiseRatio=0.03, P=24, w=0.9, validation
1.092891271 bits/symbol. Mean and signal variance are then recomputed on
all 500 training symbols. Test observations update posterior conditioning;
they do not change these fitted hyperparameters. There is no train/test
phase alignment or timestamp-continuity assumption for this stationary GP.

The MC and arima baselines fit all 500 training symbols. MC uses the
checkpoint's add-one smoothing and uniform first two forecasts. AR(2) and
AR(4) call estimate(arima(...)) and forecast(...), using uniform forecasts
for their first two/four test symbols as in the assignment. GP begins from
its training-derived Gaussian prior. All score every test symbol.

An independent training-only check compares the sequential innovations
calculation to direct GP conditional matrix equations on 30 symbols; the
maximum discrepancy is 5.33e-15. A second assertion verifies that extending
the covariance factor to future INPUT TIMES leaves the earlier factor
unchanged. No unrevealed test output enters a forecast.

REPRODUCING THE RUN

From this folder, in MATLAB R2026a with Econometrics Toolbox:
  results = unique_forecaster_gp;

PowerShell command used in the available environment:
  $env:MATLAB_PREFDIR = Join-Path (Get-Location) 'unique_forecaster_matlab_prefs'
  & 'C:\Program Files\MATLAB\R2026a\bin\matlab.exe' -batch "diary('unique_forecaster_gp_verified_run.log'); results=unique_forecaster_gp; diary off"

MATLAB startup required execution outside the sandbox in this environment.
The final successful diary contains original Symbol Machine reports for
every baseline and the GP. The model MAT stores configuration, fitted AR
objects, losses, selection table, and results. The comparison PNG plots
overall and cumulative log losses. All files use the unique_forecaster
prefix. No random sampling enters the GP result; rng(0) also fixes the
Symbol Machine's tie-breaking accuracy statistic, which is not the score.

LIMITATIONS AND THE RETAINED NEGATIVE RESULT

This is preliminary evidence on one short, ordered numeric dataset. The
Gaussian numeric interpretation is unsuitable for arbitrary text symbols.
Seasonality and Gaussian noise are assumptions; probability calibration
and performance on other datasets remain untested. Exact factorization
uses quadratic storage and cubic time and will not scale directly to the
full Dickens corpus. The discrete PMF is a normalized continuous-Gaussian
surrogate, rather than an exact categorical-GP likelihood.

An earlier static categorical clock experiment is retained as
unique_forecaster.m and unique_forecaster_results.csv. It selected period
24 and prior strength 0.1 on training-only validation, but scored
2.72233468443782 on test, behind its MC and conditional-OLS AR baselines.
It assumes test starts immediately after training on the same phase clock;
the dataset files do not supply metadata establishing that assumption.
That failed test motivated the GP family, whose grid was set before its
first test run. Consequently the test set has informed framework exploration:
a new untouched dataset would be needed for a final confirmatory claim.
